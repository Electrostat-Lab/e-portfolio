== Keywords:
Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) - Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) - Surgical Airways - Needle Cricothyrotomy - Surgical Cricothyrotomy - Needle Tracheosotomy - Surgical Tracheostomy - Respiratory Distress - Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

== Clinical Presentation:
A 35-year-old male patient was brought by the ambulance service to the ED trauma bay with light coma and disturbed conscious level; they reported that they were called by bystanders in the street as a result of a motor vehicle accident without knowing the exact mechanism, but probably a motorcycle collision.

The ATLS protocol (xABCDE) was applied by first identifying the areas of trauma with exsanguination and a trial for control of bleeding; then pelvic and spine stabilization using a _Rigid Cervical Collar_, a _Dorsolumbar Belt_, and a _Pelvic Binder_, however, it appears that the upper airway is completely lacerated with a probable partial collapse with a mandibular fracture, and severe exsanguination in the lower airway and subsequently the lungs requiring prompt assessment.

=== The following was applied in sequence:
. Aggressive Suctioning of the upper airway using SALAD technique (i.e., Suction-assisted Laryngeoscope).
. C-Spine and mandible immbolization using hand technique.
. Protection of the airway using Jaw-Thrust maneuver while stabilizing the fractured mandible.
. A trial of intubation without further sedation (i.e., Awake Intubation).

[IMPORTANT]
====
* Potential Factors Affecting the Intubation Outcome
. *Inadequate Jaw-Thrust Maneuver* — Insufficient assistance with the jaw-thrust maneuver, potentially limiting adequate exposure of the airway.
. *Inadequate Suction Technique* — Insufficient clearance of blood and secretions, with the suction tip potentially obstructing the operator's view of the vocal cords.
. *Inadequate External Laryngeal Manipulation* — Insufficient manual pressure/manipulation of the laryngeal cartilage to optimize glottic alignment and facilitate visualization.
. *Inadequate RSI Protocol for Traumatic Airway Injury* — Insufficiently adapted rapid-sequence intubation protocol for a patient with significant traumatic upper-airway bleeding/injury.
. *Inadequate Use of Intubation Facilitation Devices* — Insufficient protocol or preparation for the use of adjuncts such as a *bougie* to facilitate endotracheal tube placement.
. *Inadequate Airway Hemorrhage-Control Protocol* — Insufficient protocol regarding appropriate techniques for controlling severe upper-airway bleeding, including consideration of *balloon tamponade* where clinically indicated.
====

In this paper; a trial of redirection of the RSI/Surgical Airway protocol for the compatibility of trauma is introduced; and also is discussed as a bridge to the surgical airway in case of tramatic airway injury.

== Components of Airway Resuscitation in the trauma bay:
. Recognition of severe oropharyngeal and laryngeal bleeding sources.
. The triple airway technique (i.e., Head-tilt, Chin lift and Jaw thrust) and the vice grip handler.
. Positioning the patient to align the upper airway components' axes.
. The Suction-Assisted-Laryngeoscopy-Airway-Decontamination (S.A.L.A.D) technique.
. Premedication with protection medications against GA (e.g., Atropine or Micros of Epinephrine), and the use of airway adjuncts (e.g., Oropharyngeal Airways).
. Preoxygenation with 100% oxygen for 2-3 minutes or less.
. Administration of the induction agent (e.g., Propofol or Midazolam; best is Etomidate with the least hemodynamic adverse effects) titrated to the effect of the acutely debilitated patients (ASA Class III/IV).  
. Ensure a protocol of acute reversal of GA is present; commonly, Parasympatholytics/Cholinolytics/Vagal tone depressors (e.g., Atropine) may be administered; in addition to vasopressors and inotropes with different range of hemodynamic profiles (e.g., Norepinephrine, Ephidrine, Phenylephrine, Dopamine, Dobutamine, and Epinephrine).
. Adequate monitoring of the hemodynamic profile, neurological status and protective laryngeal reflexes, and the respiratory profile to reach the required level of sedation with the least adverse effects.
. Addition of the non-depolarizing Neuromuscular Blockers (NDNMB) per required for facilitated intubation by reduing the laryngeal tone, relaxing the laryngeothyroid muscles, and reducing the workload for respirations; thus mitgating the high oxygen demand especially in debilitated patients.
. Repeated triple airway technique, and S.A.L.A.D technique to ensure decontamination of the airway.
. Preoxygenation with 100% oxygen for 2-3 minutes or less.
. S.A.L.A.D technique with subsequent intubation with or without a bougie; maintenance of the cricoid pressure, and external manipulation (B.U.R.P technique) of the laryngeal cartilage by the operator and maintenance of the view by the assistant.

[WARNING]
====
* All indirect cholinomimetics (e.g., AChEi) are non-selective drugs; that as well as reversing the effect of the NDNMB primarily on the NMJ nicotinic receptors, they also pose adverse effects on the muscarinic receptors increasing the effect of the parasympathetic nervous system.

* Neostigmine (an AChE inhibitor) exerts a higher effect on the parasympathetic nervous system and the somatic pathways and minimal effects on the sympathetic nervous system which is slowed as a result of the ganglioinc intermediate pathways; it's also noted that in the case of weaning from ETI or reversal of NMB; the parasympathetic activation effect is an adverse effect that is unwanted in this scenario; thus, this effect could be antagonized with a minimal dosage of Atropine subsequently before neostigmine (the induction of NMB reversal) as a premedication to reduce the adverse effects of the PNS stimulation.
* At higher and toxic doses of NMB; the ion channels (Na+/K+/Ca++) are also deactivated resulting in mitigated effects of the reversal agent (e.g., Neostigmine), and subsequently higher doses of the AChEi agent (or the reversal agent) may be obligated with more adverse effects of it (e.g., Parasympathetic-induced bradycardia and hypotension).
* Very low doses of NMB with an AChEi and AChi (e.g., Neostigmine and Atropine) available at hand may be reasonable in patients with traumatic airway, but preserved conscious levels and thereby protective reflexes, however, requires airway protection due to traumatic airway injury and severe bleeding and subsequent aspiration.
